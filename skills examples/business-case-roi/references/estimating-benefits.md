# Estimating Benefits

Most proposals describe benefits as time saved or fewer errors, not as dollars. Use these formulas to convert them. **Always show the conversion** in the business case.

## Time saved
```
Annual benefit = hours saved per person per week × number of people × loaded hourly rate × 46 weeks
```
Example: 3 hrs/week × 10 analysts × $65 × 46 = **$89,700 / year**

## Fewer errors / less rework
```
Annual benefit = errors avoided per year × average cost per error
```
If the cost per error isn't known, estimate it as the hours to fix × loaded hourly rate.

## Extra revenue
```
Annual benefit = extra units or deals × average value × profit margin
```
Use **profit**, not revenue. If the margin isn't given, ask the user.

## Avoided costs
Count things you'll stop paying for, such as a replaced tool's licence, a cancelled contractor, or overtime.

## Being realistic
- **Adoption ramp-up:** new tools rarely deliver full benefits in year 1. Unless the user says otherwise, assume:
  - Year 1: **50%** of full benefit
  - Year 2: **90%**
  - Year 3 onward: **100%**
- Time saved isn't always money saved. Note in the assumptions whether freed-up time will be redeployed to other work or will actually cut costs.
